# Executive findings

## Result that most changed the judgment

The real-model A2A interaction did **not** form an accepted new capability in
this task. It exposed a real and executable conflict that simpler baselines had
already made visible: historical source-less removal can either retain
locator-global compatibility or become a null-source-only identity that protects
later sourced owners. Direct interaction refined recovery, disclosure, and
entitlement conditions, but ended at `COUNTER`, produced no patch, and was not
adopted.

This weakens the broad generative claim in collapsible local engineering tasks.
It does not refute A2A for genuinely non-collapsible authority, liability,
privacy, or acceptance.

## What the interaction produced

Six tool-less Claude owner turns produced:

- a shared composite reference identity;
- a proposed null-source legacy class;
- source-scoped supersede, auxiliary-event, and migration-evidence duties;
- severability, an infeasibility finding, disclosure and dispute rights;
- an entitlement floor that cannot disappear when one mechanism lapses.

The final action was B's `COUNTER`, not `ACCEPT`. A root-built counterfactual
passed all three null-source tests but broke two existing compatibility
expectations. The hidden implementation passed its native 44 tests but failed
all three null-source checks. This is a value conflict, not a solved formation.

See [core experiment](CORE_A2A_EXPERIMENT.md), [causality](FORMATION_CAUSALITY.md),
and [acceptance](experiments/r5-4-run-001-source-identity/acceptance.json).

  false alarm; repeated long structured-output transport closure.
- Transferable principle: an identity change is not closed until producer,
  ordered fold, projection, auxiliary events, gates, and migration evidence use

- Mechanism evidence: private vetoes can reveal a real value conflict and form

  adoption, necessity, or positive net value.

## Which simpler approach performed better

The static builder cost USD 0.540 and found the main composite-identity defect,
although its patch was malformed and semantically incomplete. Direct A2A cost
USD 1.344 across six calls, produced no signed contract or patch, and required
more verification. Fixed central queries cost less than A2A and exposed both
owner policies, but the builder transport failed twice. For this task, the
observed A2A increment did not exceed its coordination cost.

## Unknowns that remain actionable

- The real mixed legacy corpus was not measured. A source-less removal may or
  may not coexist with explicitly sourced rows.
- The authorized owner has not chosen compatibility versus sourced isolation
  for that mixed case.
- A shorter/streaming central synthesis was not run under this protocol.
- This task did not contain genuinely non-collapsible organizational authority.

## Highest-value next action

Measure the legacy corpus and obtain an owner acceptance decision before
changing semantics. Then either run a shorter, independently preregistered
central synthesis or select a task whose authority/liability cannot safely be
collapsed. More rounds on the same unresolved value choice would add messages,
not resolving power.
